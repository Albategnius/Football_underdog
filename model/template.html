    Does the radar work?

    Each past match was predicted using only data from before that week, then checked against the result. If the radar is honest, games it calls 30% upsets should end in upsets about 30% of the time.

    How it works

    • A Dixon–Coles goals model: each team gets an attack and a defence rating, plus a home advantage. Those ratings give expected goals for both sides and a probability for every scoreline.
    • Trained on 11 Premier League seasons (2015/16 onwards). Recent matches count more: a result from a year ago carries half the weight of last weekend's.
    • Newly promoted clubs start with a measured "promoted team" penalty, then earn their own rating as results come in.
    • The underdog is the side with the lower win probability. An upset means the underdog wins outright. Draws are shown separately.
    • Goals only. No shots, injuries, line-ups or betting odds, so it can't know about a missing striker or a manager sacking.
    Fixtures and results: openfootball. Model and backtest run on 10 Oct 2026. For information and fun, not betting advice.
